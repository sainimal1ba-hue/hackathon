## THE SLAIN

- **PRIMORDIA (29)** — A team with no ML training experience presenting a training run to 150 non-ML students: if it converges it's a tutorial, if it doesn't it's a funeral; the demo IS the model, and the model will embarrass them.
- **VOX NOCTURNA (35)** — Live mic + noisy hall + offline Ollama RAM + PyAutoGUI coordinate roulette = demo roulette; the dead-cable moment comes only after three luck dependencies say yes first, on the most crowded lane in the building.
- **MIRRORBREAKER (36)** — The "vaccine" side cannot be proven effective on stage (you'd have to train the cloner live), the detector side is heavy inference on hackathon laptops, and dozens of deepfake clones split the audience — the red flash is a gamble, not a demo.
- **CHAINHOUND (38)** — "Follow the REAL money" through Etherscan/RPC/bridge endpoints in an OFFLINE venue: the premise is a third-party API dependency, so the "live manhunt" is a Dockerized cache replay wearing a detective's coat — and rate limits kill the dress rehearsal too.
- **LEDGERKEEPER (39)** — The most competent corpse of the night: F24 9, FIT 9, and CROWD 3 — a flawlessly executed budget app drowning among fifteen near-identical finance CRUD clones; "people vote for themselves" fails when the crowd has already seen themselves fourteen times. (Tie at 39 broken against it: AMPERA's tamper-theater prop is a true twist; "polish" is not.)

## SEMIFINALS

**PS-WEB-01 HEROKILL (46).** The case: this is the only PS where the 24 hours buy polish instead of learning — CI, 98 green tests, and Docker deploys are already this team's day job, so F24/FIT sit at the ceiling while AI and blockchain rivals spend the night taming stacks they've never touched. The wow is self-inflicted sabotage — poisoned commit deployed, health checks screaming, timeline scrubber rolling back to green, murdered container resurrecting — fully seeded, deterministic, local: no mic, no internet, no attacker, no inference, precisely the dependency list that will kill half the arena. The risk: "devops" can read unsexy to a student crowd, Docker-in-Docker can brick the demo box, and "hand the room a live URL" quietly assumes network access an offline hall won't give 200 phones. The counter: host-socket mounting with a pre-baked pristine image, a Vercel-grade UI dressing the plumbing, and the sabotage as the opening act — the rollback scrubber is the sex appeal.

**PS-CYB-02 THE WARDEN (45).** The case: a five-minute heist movie — poisoned email hijacks the assistant, red alert, payload blocked, forensic replay — with the safest failure envelope in the tournament: deny-by-default means even a miss plays on stage as "blocked by policy," which is a win. The injection corpus locked in CI makes the 98-test discipline a visible bouncer, and Node middleware is native soil. The risk: the story's star is an AI assistant the team must fake offline (scripted persona or tiny local model), which dims the "watch AI get told NO" magic; a security HUD is conceptually drier than resurrection theater. The counter: prompt injection is a meme the whole room knows in 2026 — make the payload personal (read the .env, mail the payroll) and have an audience volunteer fire the poisoned email so the crowd commits the crime and the Warden stops it.

**PS-WEB-02 DURABILIS (44).** The case: kill -9 mid-workflow with the engine resurrecting at step 7 of 9, the email provably fired exactly once (one message in a local inbox, checkable on screen), plus an audience-triggered chaos button — the most physically bulletproof wow available, all on their own hardware, with the test suite proving resume correctness live. FIT 9, zero new stack. The risk: "workflow automation" is the n8n-clone lane, and exactly-once is a research-grade trap that must be honestly downgraded to at-least-once + idempotency keys, softening the headline; a node-canvas is a familiar sight by demo hour ten. The counter: make the crash the hero — ghost-replay scrubbing to the exact moment of death with visible state — and let the audience choose the victim: resurrection beats configuration every time.

## THE CHAMPION

**Winner: PS-WEB-01 HEROKILL (Mini-Cloud Hosting Platform). Runner-up: PS-CYB-02 THE WARDEN.** (DURABILIS rides one kill behind as the dark horse.)

**The decisive argument.** HEROKILL wins because it is the only proposal where all six axes point the same direction at once. F24 and FIT are near-ceiling: the platform is literally the team's existing discipline turned into a product — they already maintain the codebase, the 98 tests, the Actions CI, the Docker deploys — so every hour of the 24 buys theater while rivals spend theirs learning Ollama, Solidity, or CRDT undo semantics cold. The WOW is a resurrection the team controls end-to-end: they deploy their own poison, the health checks scream, the timeline scrubs back to green, and the killed container reboots — seeded, deterministic, rehearsed, and requiring none of the things that kill demos in that hall: no live mic, no venue Wi-Fi, no third-party API, no attacker who must show up, no model inference on a hackathon laptop. CROWD is the quietest lane available — Docker-heavy platform work repels casuals, and the few clans that dare mostly drown in container-in-container hell this team sidesteps by mounting the host socket. DIFF is open ground: every rival ships a deploy button; the rollback scrubber and per-PR preview URLs are unclaimed. And on the criterion this arena underestimates, it scores maximally — "no green, no live" puts the test suite on stage as a bouncer, discipline as a visible feature, not hygiene. The Warden loses by a single point only because a red banner over a proxy is one dramatic beat, while HEROKILL packs sabotage, rollback, and resurrection into five minutes the audience can watch like a match.

**THE ANGLE** — We build the push-to-deploy mini-cloud every infra clan will build + a scrubbable resurrection timeline that sabotages our own app and auto-heals it in front of the room — and the moment a poisoned deploy screams red, rolls itself back to green, and the murdered container reboots under the crowd's eyes is the moment that takes the audience vote.

**The fatal dependency and its fallback.** The weakness is the finale, not the core: "hand the room a live URL" assumes network access an offline hall with shared/unknown Wi-Fi won't reliably give 200 phones, and live SSL/domain minting needs Let's Encrypt — i.e., the internet. The fallback that preserves the crown: go projector-first — deploy from a pre-baked pristine repo on the team's own box, cut the audience-phone moment to a judge's loaner laptop deploying on the same machine/LAN, pre-mint a wildcard certificate with local DNS overrides so "custom domain + HTTPS" stays in the show, and let the sabotage-rollback-resurrection act — which needs nothing but their own hardware — carry the vote.
